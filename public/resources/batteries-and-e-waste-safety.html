Batteries and e-waste safety

Lithium-ion batteries are the main hazard in old electronics. A few habits keep them safe until they're recycled.

Signs of a damaged battery

  • The case or trackpad bulges, or the lid no longer sits flat.
  • The device is hot when it's off, or has a sweet or chemical smell.
  • The battery leaks or has visible dents or punctures.

If you see any of these, stop using and charging the device. Don't try to puncture, flatten or squeeze it.

What to do with a swollen battery

  1. Unplug the device and power it down. Move it away from anything flammable, ideally onto a hard surface such as a tile or concrete floor.
  2. Don't mail it or put it in a car on a hot day.
  3. Take it to a battery drop-off that accepts damaged batteries, and tell them it's damaged. Some programs won't take damaged cells; call ahead. The Call2Recycle locator is a good start.

Hardware Club can't accept loose or swollen batteries, so please don't bring them to a handoff.

Storing old devices

  • Keep them somewhere cool and dry, away from heat sources and direct sun.
  • Tape over the terminals of loose batteries, or bag each one separately.
  • Don't leave devices charging unattended for days.

Don't put electronics in the trash

Electronics contain materials that shouldn't be buried, and batteries have started fires in garbage trucks and recycling facilities. Disposal rules and collection programs vary by community and change over time, so check with your county, city, or the state environment department (EGLE). The local directory links the official sources, and the EPA has general guidance.

Hardware Club doesn't take

CRT monitors and TVs, printers, kitchen and household appliances, and loose or swollen batteries.
